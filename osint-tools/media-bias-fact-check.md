### What does Media Bias/Fact Check do?

Media Bias/Fact Check (MBFC) maintains a large database of media sources and evaluates them using a published methodology covering areas such as political bias, factual reporting, sourcing, transparency, and one-sidedness/omission. Its current methodology requires reviewers to examine at least 10 headlines and 5 full stories for a source, with additional research where necessary.

It also provides a daily fact-check search that links to third-party fact-checking organisations such as PolitiFact, Snopes and [FactCheck.org](http://factcheck.org).

**Note:** MBFC itself states that its evaluations are subjective, evidence-based editorial assessments and that there is no universally accepted scientific standard for measuring media bias.

**The lowdown:** You can use the platform to understand how a publication has been assessed before relying on its reporting, identify potential sourcing or transparency issues, and find supporting fact-check material.

### Use in Reporting

Media Bias/Fact Check can be used to:

* Assess the published background of a media outlet before relying on its reporting.
* Identify documented sourcing, transparency and editorial issues associated with a source.
* Compare the assessment of different media sources covering the same event.
* Investigate the factual-reporting history documented in a source profile.
* Locate third-party fact checks associated with claims or media reporting.
* Identify ownership, funding and editorial information where available.
* Provide contextual information when explaining why an investigator sought additional corroboration.
* Cross-check media-source assessments against other source-rating organisations and primary evidence.
* Support media-literacy and misinformation research.

MBFC data has also been used in academic research. For example, research published in[ Online Information Review ](https://www.emerald.com/oir/article/48/5/908/1220830/Polarization-and-reliability-of-news-sources-in)combined MBFC data with a large Wikipedia citation dataset to study political polarisation and factual reliability of news sources.&#x20;

| **Capabilities**                                                               | **Limitations**                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Provides searchable profiles for thousands of media and information sources.   | Ratings are assessments of sources, not individual articles in every case.                                               |
| Examines bias, factual reporting, sourcing and transparency.                   | MBFC acknowledges that measuring political bias involves human judgment.                                                 |
| Provides detailed reports explaining the reasoning behind source assessments.  | Its methodology is primarily tailored to the US political context, which can limit direct comparisons across countries.  |
| Links to or draws on third-party fact-checking evidence.                       | A source rating does not prove that a particular article is accurate or inaccurate.                                      |
| Can be used to compare source characteristics during an investigation.         | Ratings can change as sources, evidence and MBFC methodology change.                                                     |
| Has been used as a dataset in academic misinformation and media research.      | Researchers have raised methodological criticisms about using MBFC ratings as objective or authoritative measurements.   |

### Summary

Media Bias/Fact Check is a source-assessment and media-research tool that provides structured evaluations of media outlets and other information sources. It’s particularly useful during verification in the OSINT workflow, where investigators can assess the background and reporting characteristics of a source before deciding how much corroboration is appropriate.&#x20;

**Important Note:** Its own methodology describes the ratings as structured human assessments, while independent researchers have raised criticisms about their suitability for rigorous academic use.&#x20;

### Ownership

American Independent media-rating organisation founded in 2015 with [Dave Van Zandt](https://www.linkedin.com/in/dave-van-zandt-09421b169/) identified as founder and primary editor.

### Ethical Considerations

* Do not treat an MBFC rating as proof that an individual article is accurate or inaccurate.
* Use the underlying evidence and methodology rather than relying only on a headline rating.
* Be careful when describing politically sensitive ratings; distinguish MBFC's assessment from independently established facts.
* Consider the geographical limitations of a methodology developed primarily around the US political environment.
* Cross-check important assessments with other source-rating organisations and primary evidence.
* Record the date of the MBFC assessment because profiles and ratings can change.
