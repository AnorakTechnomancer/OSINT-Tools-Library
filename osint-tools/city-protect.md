### What does City Protect do?

City Protect provides a public-facing way to access crime and public-safety information published by participating law-enforcement agencies. Its map interface allows users to locate agencies and explore incidents using geographic, date and incident-type filters.

The platform can also provide additional community-engagement functions, including incident-update subscriptions, anonymous tips, online police reporting and other agency-specific services.

**The lowdown:** It’s particularly useful when an investigation requires local US incident data that may not be easily discoverable through a general web search.&#x20;

### Cookies:&#x20;

City Protect uses several first-party cookies relating to cookie-consent preferences, session management and website analytics. The notice\_\* and optout\_domains\_pc cookies record privacy/consent preferences and interactions with the site's cookie notice. TAsessionID is a unique session identifier used to maintain or recognise a visitor session. utag\_main is associated with Tealium and records pseudonymous session and page-activity information for analytics/tag management.

### Use in Reporting

City Protect can be used to:

* Verify that a publicly reported incident appears in the participating agency's published crime data.
* Identify reported incidents associated with a particular location or geographical area.
* Investigate incidents occurring within a defined date or time range.
* Filter incidents by publicly available incident categories, such as assault or other crime types.
* Build preliminary timelines of reported public-safety incidents.
* Identify clusters or recurring incident patterns that may warrant further investigation.
* Cross-reference incident information with police statements, court records, local news reports and other independent sources.
* Support investigations into local crime patterns, public-safety events and incidents involving particular locations.

As an example use case, the [University of Kansas' official Annual Security and Fire Safety Report](https://civilrights.ku.edu/sites/civilrights/files/documents/Reports/University_of_Kansas_Lawrence_Clery_ASR_2022.pdf) describes how crime information was uploaded to CrimeReports/CityProtect and explains that CityProtect provided a map-based system for viewing reported crimes over selected dates.&#x20;

| **Capabilities**                                                                                     | **Limitations**                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Provides a central public interface for participating law-enforcement agencies.                      | Coverage is dependent on agencies participating in City Protect.                                           |
| Allows crime incidents to be explored geographically using an interactive map.                       | Not every police agency or jurisdiction is necessarily represented.                                        |
| Provides filters for date ranges, days, times and incident categories.                               | Published incident data may be sanitised, generalised or limited compared with underlying police records.  |
| Can help identify individual reported incidents and broader local patterns.                          | Incident classifications and publication practices can vary between agencies.                              |
| Provides incident-update functionality for selected searches/areas.                                  | Data may not be real-time and historical coverage can vary.                                                |
| Can connect members of the public with additional agency services such as tips or online reporting.  | A City Protect listing does not independently establish that an allegation or incident has been proven.    |

### Summary

City Protect is a useful US public-safety and crime-map resource for finding publicly released incident information from participating law-enforcement agencies. It works best in the discovery, collection, verification and analysis stages of the OSINT workflow but shouldn’t be used as a substitute for obtaining the underlying police report or other primary evidence.

### Ethical Considerations

* Do not assume that a reported incident means an individual is guilty of an offence.
* Treat incident classifications as published categories rather than definitive legal findings.
* Verify significant claims against primary or independent sources.
* Avoid publishing unnecessary personal information about victims, witnesses or other private individuals.
* Take particular care with information relating to sexual offences, minors and vulnerable people.
* Do not infer crime rates or trends from an incomplete set of participating agencies without explaining the limitations of the dataset.
