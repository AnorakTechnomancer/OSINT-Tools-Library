### What does Russian Aggression Incident Log do?

Russian Aggression Incident Log provides four dedicated views to investigate Russian hybrid operations: an Incident Log of verified events (cable disruptions, GPS jamming, drone incursions), a Tactical Map with toggleable overlays for tracked spy ships (Yantar), shadow tankers, and subsea pipelines, and vessel Dossiers.&#x20;

An Intelligence Analytics dashboard visualises monthly incident frequency trends, category breakdowns, and geographic NATO front distributions using interactive charts. Built-in AI generates automated SITREP briefings detailing current escalation risks and tactical patterns, while a natural-language AI search bar lets you ask questions to uncover timelines, correlations, and specific threat actors across the entire database.

**The lowdown:** The site provides a structured timeline of reported incidents involving Russian hostile activity towards NATO countries and partners.&#x20;

### Use in Reporting

**Russian Aggression Incident Log can be used to:**

* Locate reported examples of Russian military, political, cyber, maritime, air and hybrid activity.
* Establish chronological sequences of incidents involving a particular country or region.
* Generate investigative leads.
* Compare incident descriptions with official statements from governments, defence ministries and NATO.
* Examine recurring activity across countries, dates and incident categories.
* Provide a starting point for conflict, security and geopolitical investigations.

**Real-World Investigation Examples Tracked in the Tool:**

* **Baltic Subsea Cable Severing (Yi Peng 3 / C-Lion1 & BCS Cables, Nov 2024)**

Application: Cross-referencing subsea fiber coordinates (Finland–Germany C-Lion1 and Sweden–Lithuania BCS cables) against the track of Chinese-flagged bulk carrier Yi Peng 3 departing Ust-Luga, Russia with Russian crew members on board.

Reference Reporting: Financial Times: Chinese ship probed over severed Baltic seabed cables | Reuters Investigation<br>

* **The Balticconnector Pipeline & Telecom Ruptures (Newnew Polar Bear, Oct 2023)**

Application: Tracking spatial intersections between underwater gas infrastructure in the Gulf of Finland and anchor-dragging commercial vessels loitering in Russian-adjacent corridors.

Reference Reporting: Yle News: Finnish police confirm anchor damage to Balticconnector<br>

* **Baltic & Nordic GNSS Jamming Epidemic ("Baltic Jammer", 2023–Present)**

Application: Mapping GPS signal degradation clusters affecting thousands of commercial flights and civilian vessels across Poland, the Baltics, and southern Sweden, traced back to electronic warfare arrays in Kaliningrad.

Reference Reporting: Bellingcat / The Guardian: Baltic GPS jamming surge



* **Russian GUGI Spy Ship Loitering (Yantar & Admiral Vladimirsky, 2022–2024)**

Application: Mapping hydrographic research and "special purpose" Russian vessels equipped with deep-sea submersibles loitering over North Sea wind farms and Irish Sea trans-Atlantic internet cables.

Reference Reporting: DR / SVT / NRK Nordic Public Broadcasters: "The Russian Spy Ships / Shadow War" Joint Investigation



* **Danube Delta Drone Incursions (Romania & Moldova, 2023–2024)**

Application: Logging Shahed-136 drone wreckage and border violations adjacent to Ukrainian Izmail and Reni ports to document NATO border spillover frequency.

Reference Reporting: Radio Free Europe / Digi24: Russian drone debris on Romanian territory.

### Summary

Russian Aggression Incident Log’s main value is in helping investigators quickly find incidents, construct timelines, and generate leads that can then be verified through primary and reputable secondary sources. It fits best within the collection, discovery and analysis stages of the OSINT workflow and should therefore generally be regarded as a lead-generation and cross-referencing resource rather than a standalone evidential source.

### Ownership

The platform describes itself as an independent OSINT intelligence feed. Ownership is not clearly identified publicly.&#x20;

### Ethical Considerations

* Do not assume that an incident's classification establishes Russian responsibility.
* Check original sources and use primary documentation wherever possible.
* Clearly identify whether information comes from an underlying source or the database's own interpretation.
* Keep the incident ID, URL, access date and original sources used during verification.
* Do not republish personal or operationally sensitive information without a legitimate reporting reason.
* Consider source bias by taking the site's stated purpose and editorial approach into account when assessing its categorisations.
