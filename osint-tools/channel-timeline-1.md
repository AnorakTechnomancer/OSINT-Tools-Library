### What does Twitter Trending Archive do?

Twitter Trending Archive provides historical records of X/Twitter trending topics where you can select a country and date and retrieve archived trend information.&#x20;

Individual country/date pages provide fields including: most tweeted, longest trending, highest position/rank, total time appearing in the trends, daily rank, and highest tweet volume.

**The lowdown:** It can provide a historical reference point that is difficult to obtain directly from X, particularly when investigating events that occurred months or years earlier.

### How to Use:

**1. Select the country or worldwide trend archive you want to investigate. Then select the relevant date and review the topics recorded for that day.**

These cookies relate primarily to website analytics, security, consent and visitor/session management, rather than the underlying X/Twitter trend data.&#x20;

### Use in Reporting

Twitter Trending Archive can be useful for:

* Establishing whether a hashtag or topic appeared in X/Twitter's trending lists for a particular country and date.
* Reconstructing historical timelines showing when a topic entered, remained in or disappeared from the trend lists.
* Investigating coordinated or potentially manipulated trending activity by comparing the timing, duration, ranking and volume of hashtags.
* Comparing trends across countries or geographical locations to identify differences in the visibility of particular topics.
* Supporting investigations into political campaigns, information operations, disinformation and online mobilisation by providing historical trend data.
* Supporting academic and analytical research, including research into political communication, conflict-related discourse and social-media attention. Published research has directly used Archive Tweets data for these purposes.
* Corroborating investigative findings by providing an independent historical record of a trend, while directing readers to the underlying X/Twitter material or additional sources where possible.

The archive has been used in published research. For example,[ a study of social-media attention surrounding Russia's war in Ukraine](https://freepolicybriefs.org/tag/russian-aggression/) used data from the archive covering 62 countries from July 2021 to December 2023, collecting the five highest-volume and five longest-trending topics for each country/day.

[A 2024 Digital Witness Lab methodology also used the site](https://www.digitalwitnesslab.org/outputs/trend-alert-methodology) to establish whether hashtags associated with political campaign messages in India were trending on X/Twitter during specified periods. The researchers noted that the archive provides trend observations at 30-minute intervals and used it to identify which campaign hashtags had appeared in the trend lists.

| **Capabilities**                                                                                                                | **Limitations**                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Allows researchers to examine X/Twitter trends for previous dates and countries.                                                | It primarily records trend info rather than providing a complete archive of individual Tweets and accounts.                                                  |
| Provides info about when topics appeared and, where available, how long they remained in the trend list.                        | Trend observations are available at 30-minute intervals so very short-lived trends may be missed.                                                            |
| Allows trends to be examined across different countries and worldwide.                                                          | Available countries and historical coverage may vary, and country trend data should not automatically be treated as representative of an entire population.  |
| Some entries provide Tweet-volume information associated with a trending topic.                                                 | A high Tweet volume does not demonstrate that activity was organic, coordinated, manipulated or responsible for a trend.                                     |
| Can provide reproducible source material for research into political communication, conflicts, campaigns and online attention.  | Published users have identified gaps in the archive, including missing observations during parts of April 2024.                                              |
| Historical country/date pages can be accessed without an account.                                                               | The archive relies on X/Twitter data and its own collection process, so changes to the platform or API may affect coverage and continuity.                   |

### Summary

Twitter Trending Archive is particularly useful after identifying a hashtag, topic or online campaign, when you need to establish whether and when that topic appeared in X/Twitter's trending lists. It therefore best fits in the verification and analysis stages of the OSINT workflow, with a secondary role in discovery. However, it’s not a comprehensive archive of Tweets or accounts, and its time-based observations can miss short-lived trends.&#x20;

As always, significant findings should be corroborated against the original X/Twitter material and other independent sources.

### Ethical Considerations

* Do not assume that a trending hashtag was artificially or maliciously generated simply because its activity appears unusual.
* Distinguish correlation between a campaign/event and a trend from evidence that one caused the other.
* Treat Tweet-volume figures as contextual indicators rather than definitive measures of public support.
* Consider the archive's sampling interval and known data gaps when reconstructing timelines.
* Be careful when using historical trend data to make claims about public opinion or political behaviour.
* Record the country, date, time period, archive URL and access date when citing the archive so that the analysis can be reproduced.
