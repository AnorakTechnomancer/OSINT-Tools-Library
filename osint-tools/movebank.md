### What does Movebank do?

Movebank provides infrastructure for researchers to manage, share, analyse and archive animal movement and animal-borne sensor data. The platform can handle data from GPS, Argos Doppler, radio and acoustic telemetry, solar geolocators, bird rings and natural markers, as well as other sensors carried by animals.

Arguably the most relevant component is the Tracking Data Map, which allows users to search for studies and view animal tracks that researchers have made publicly accessible. Studies can be searched by keywords, taxa, locations and other criteria.

Public datasets can sometimes be downloaded in formats suitable for further analysis, including CSV and formats compatible with tools such as Excel, Google Earth and ArcGIS.

**The lowdown:** It’s a scientific research and data-sharing infrastructure with public datasets that can be repurposed for investigative and geospatial research. <br>

### Use in Reporting

Movebank can be used to:

* Locate publicly available animal-tracking studies relevant to an investigation.
* Investigate the movements of tagged animals over time.
* Examine migration routes and geographic patterns.
* Establish temporal and geographic context around wildlife or environmental events.
* Identify studies conducted in a particular geographic area.
* Examine the relationship between animal movements and habitats or environmental conditions.
* Download permitted tracking datasets for GIS and statistical analysis.
* Compare animal movements across different studies or time periods.
* Support conservation and wildlife investigations with primary research datasets.
* Provide a documented source for maps, movement analysis and quantitative findings.

Movebank data has been used in various published research and conservation investigations involving animal mortality, migration, movement patterns, demography, habitat use and environmental change. A notable example is [Kays et al. (2022),](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.13767) which used Movebank for near-real-time mortality detection and a citizen-science investigation of suspected animal deaths.

### Summary

Movebank is a free scientific animal-movement database and research platform rather than a conventional OSINT tool. It best fits in the discovery and analysis stages of the OSINT workflow and is valuable for locating animal-tracking datasets and analysing geographic and time-series movement patterns. This can be particularly relevant to environmental journalism, wildlife investigations, conservation research and geospatial analysis.&#x20;

**Important note:** Movebank does not own the underlying research data. Individual researchers control access and licensing, and public availability varies from study to study.&#x20;

### Ownership

According to the website, Movebank is “managed as a free online research platform hosted by the[ Max Planck Institute of Animal Behavior](https://www.ab.mpg.de/) in coordination with the[ North Carolina Museum of Natural Sciences](http://naturalsciences.org/), the[ Ohio State University](https://ceg.osu.edu/), the[ Senckenberg Society for Nature Research](https://www.senckenberg.de/en/), and the[ University of Konstanz](https://www.uni-konstanz.de/en/).”&#x20;

### Ethical Considerations

* Check the study's licence and access conditions before downloading or publishing data.
* Attribute the original researchers and dataset according to the study's requirements.
* Do not assume that publicly viewable tracks can automatically be republished in full.
* Consider whether publishing precise coordinates could endanger animals or sensitive habitats.
* Preserve the study identifier, data owner, licence and retrieval date.
* Treat location data as measurements rather than automatically assuming they are error-free and be aware that Movebank itself warns that datasets can contain erroneous measurements, location outliers and incomplete information.
